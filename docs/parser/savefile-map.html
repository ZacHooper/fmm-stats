Read this first: the drift boundary

Everything below ~20 MB is near-static: a reference-half offset from any save of the same career is good to about ±100 KB, close enough to seek to and confirm with the table's header. Everything above ~34 MB moves by megabytes and not monotonically. It sawtooths, because the seasonal transfer band fills by ~2.5 MB each season and is wiped every July.

Four kinds of region

Ask one question of every region: what happens to it as the career goes on? The answer tells you whether an offset or a count from another save still holds, and whether an empty-looking stretch is missing data or room not yet used.

Dead end

Club-records and our matches each once passed for a fixed pool. Neither is: club-records holds 1,057 clubs on every Frem save while its size grows 1,224,589 → 1,345,853 bytes from 2023 to 2030, and our matches grow with the match count and empty on the rollover day. To tell a pool from a structure that grows by insertion, follow one entity across saves (one club's row), never a region's size. And check a "constant" on a third save: two that agree prove nothing.

The July rollover, measured

frem-2026-06-29 → frem-2026-07-02 loses 3,004,689 bytes. Anchor-matching one save into the other (a 48-byte non-filler sample every 64 KB, its unique match in the other save, the offset delta) puts every byte of that loss in one of two places.

The transfer band accounts for its step on its own: across that pair it goes 3.32 MB → 0.79 MB (6,877 → 1,720 rows carrying contract terms), a 2.53 MB collapse against a 2.54 MB measured deletion. Our matches go from 60 anchors to 0.

The rollover day drifts

The engine advances a 365-day season counter, not a calendar date, so every leap day slips the rollover one day earlier: 30 June / 1 July in 2021–2023, 29 June from 2024, 28/29 June from 2028. A save taken even two days late has already lost the season's transfers and matches.

Dead end

Ranking a gap by its printable fraction. Compressed data is 37.1% printable by construction (95 of 256 byte values), so the archive once ranked as "the most text-dense unparsed span" for a false reason. Rank by block entropy (scripts/audit/entropy_profile.py): filler ~1.4 bits/byte, records 3–6, compressed 7.9+.

The transfer band row, 143 bytes

Every transfer negotiation of the season so far, one row per bid. [u32 65,000][u32 count][2 B], then count rows of 143 bytes, row k holding k at +109. The 65,000 is a capacity; the count is exact. Read a row as a bid, never as a transfer: only the 0d row holds the agreed fee.

Quirk

The row frame starts 2 bytes after the count. Framed from the index instead (index at +8, tid at +48), each row's selling club sits at +0 of the next row, which looks like a column offset. It is a framing error: start from the header.

The sicomps archive

The save ends in a zstd archive: a chain of [u32 stored][zstd frame] members and a directory that names them, ending on the file's last byte. Every member opens [03][01]['tad.'] (.dat reversed). Decompressed sizes on frem-2026-06-11, 6.58 MB in all from 1.30 MB stored:

The archive is current state: fix_man is a two-calendar-year window and the rules are this season's. A 2026 save knows nothing of 2021. History comes from keeping snapshots.

FMM27 stores the same archive without the length prefixes: bare zstd frames back to back, and a 9-byte directory header with no size, its frame running to EOF (168 members on truro-2026-07-25). The markdown map has the FMM27 career-half offsets.

Reproducing the structural rows

  1. Contract grid: scan for [tid u32][0x01][wage u16][6×00][doy u16][year u16], keep hits whose gaps are exact multiples of 83; the run's extent ÷ 83 is the slot count.
  2. Transfer band: [u32 65,000][u32 count] in front of row 0 (the row frame starts 2 bytes after the count), and every row k holds k as a u32 at +109.
  3. Player-progress pool: two consecutive unused 70-byte rows, then walk both ways row by row.
  4. Club-records extent: the u16 club count after the history pool, walked row by row.
  5. Player-list blocks: an unused list (14 01 00 0a 00 at tail +121 of 100 snapshots, 200 bytes apart), then walk list by list.
  6. The archive: the first 28 b5 2f fd (zstd magic) in the file.
  7. Drift class: compare any two saves. The reference half moves in tens of KB, the career half in megabytes.
